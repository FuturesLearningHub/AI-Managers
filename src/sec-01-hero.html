Why the university is asking managers to lead this way. The institutional why · Crescere aude

Vanderbilt will define the great university of the 21st Century and be it.

That is Chancellor Diermeier’s institutional vision, one sentence with no hedge in it. The how is equally blunt: operate at uncommon speed, agility, and scale to address society’s challenges through education and research, and help reclaim the essential role of leading research universities in driving American prosperity9. Our motto has always been the dare, Crescere aude, dare to grow, and the dare lands on managers like you, one ordinary week at a time.

Areas of Focus

Three ways Vanderbilt delivers on the vision

1

Exceptional Core Operations

With speed, agility and scale, Vanderbilt will distinguish itself with innovative and impactful capabilities to ensure that we have the talent, resources and reputation necessary to deliver on our vision.

2

Bold Strategic Initiatives

With “exceptional core operations” as our fuel, Vanderbilt will pursue increasingly bold initiatives that enable and enhance the reach and impact of our education and research mission.

3

Values Leadership

Through strategic advocacy efforts and modeling the role of an essential research university, Vanderbilt will stimulate industry change and combat existential threats to our education and research mission.

How this course delivers

Leading with AI puts Exceptional Core Operations inside the manager’s week. The hours these habits give back are hours you spend on the people and the work only you can do, and the four anchors keep the speed from costing us judgment.

The reputation flywheel

Reputation and resources feed each other in a loop. Reputation attracts talent, funding, partnerships, and access, which build the reputation. The university spins that flywheel with execution with ambition, values-driven leadership, and athletics momentum. Here is your place in it: values-driven leadership shows up in how a manager writes, coaches, and decides. A manager who edits the draft into their own voice, prepares before the 1:1, and owns the call is what it looks like on an ordinary Tuesday.
