Which tool, for which moment.

Vanderbilt gives you three approved tools, and managers lose more time choosing between them than using them. You do not need to compare features. One question sorts almost every case: how sensitive is the material you are about to paste in? Read the three cards, then try the drill underneath.

Tool 01

Vanderbilt Amplify

Use whenThe content involves internal Vanderbilt information at Data Levels 1 to 3. You sign in with your VU account, and your data is not used to train the models2.
Best forSummarizing internal documents, policy-adjacent memos, custom assistants, and the email, scheduling, and note-taker features in Amplify 2.01.
Watch outNever upload Level 4, PHI, or HIPAA data. Amplify being approved for internal work does not make it approved for everything.
1 to 3data levels covered

This is the when-in-doubt choice. If you cannot classify what you are holding, it belongs here or nowhere.

Tool 02

Microsoft Copilot

Use whenThe source material already lives in your VU Microsoft 365 workspace: a Teams meeting, an Outlook thread, an Excel file.
Best forMeeting recaps in Teams, pulling action items out of a transcript, replying to an email thread in Outlook, cleaning up a spreadsheet5.
Watch outCopilot inherits your permissions. If a sensitive file is over-shared in SharePoint, Copilot can surface it. Confirm the sharing before you ask.
M365where the source already is

The advantage is proximity. You are not pasting anything; the material is already inside the tool.

Tool 03

ChatGPT EDU

Use whenYou want the strongest general-purpose model for reasoning and long-form drafting, and nothing internal or personal is involved. Conversations are not used to train OpenAI’s models4.
Best forBrainstorming, long drafts on public or generic material, non-sensitive analysis, custom GPTs.
Watch outDo not paste confidential Vanderbilt data here. Those prompts go to Amplify3.
0internal records that belong here

Approved to a lower level than the other two. That is the whole distinction worth remembering.

Data classification decides. Level 4, PHI, and HIPAA data go into none of these tools, information about children goes into none of them either, and when you are unsure, it goes to Amplify.

Plain English: a data level is Vanderbilt’s label for how sensitive a piece of information is, from 1 (public) to 4 (the most restricted). PHI is protected health information. HIPAA is the federal law that governs it. Every term this course uses is listed again in the appendix.
Try it · 45 seconds

Three cases. Pick the tool.

Nothing is scored or sent anywhere. Pick an answer and the reasoning appears underneath.

Case 1. A Teams meeting just ended and you want a recap with owners and due dates. The transcript is sitting in Teams.

Copilot. The transcript already lives in your Microsoft 365 workspace, so nothing has to be pasted anywhere. Amplify would also be safe, and it is the right call if the note-taker is where your transcript actually lives. ChatGPT EDU is the wrong home for an internal meeting.

Case 2. You want to sharpen an opening paragraph for a talk about leadership. No Vanderbilt specifics, no names, nothing internal.

ChatGPT EDU. Nothing internal or personal is involved, so the strongest general-purpose model is a fair choice. Amplify would work too and costs you nothing to prefer. The point of this case is that the lower-level tool is genuinely fine when the material is generic.

Case 3. A team member disclosed a medical condition and you want help thinking through a schedule accommodation.

None of them. Health information about a person is protected health information, and no approved tool takes it. Take the accommodation to HR and your HR business partner. If you use AI at all here, it is to think about a generic scheduling question with no person attached, no condition named, and no detail that could identify anyone.

Together

Run the three cases as a room, hands up for each tool before the answer shows. Then take one case from the floor: somebody names a real thing they were about to paste somewhere, and the room sorts it.

Pause + reflect

Think of the last thing you pasted into an AI tool. Would it pass Case 3? If you are not sure how it was classified, that uncertainty is the answer: it belonged in Amplify.
