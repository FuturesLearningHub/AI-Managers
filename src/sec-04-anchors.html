Module 01 of 5. About two minutes. Module 01 · Ethical and inclusive practice

Four anchors hold the frame.

AI drafts fast and it sounds certain, which is exactly the problem. Certainty is the model’s writing style, not evidence that it is right. The leader holds the frame, and four questions from Harvard Business Review’s AI leadership guidance hold it for you6. Run any output through these before you act on it. It takes about fifteen seconds once you know them.

Anchor 01
Authority

You own the final draft, not the tool. Edit it into your voice before it leaves you. If a message goes out sounding like nobody wrote it, your team will notice, and what they will conclude is that you did not think about them.

Ask: am I editing this in my voice?

Anchor 02
Purpose

Tie the output to a goal you can name, near-term or long-term. If it does not advance the goal, it is noise, and polished noise takes up more of your team’s attention than rough signal does.

Ask: which goal does this serve?

Anchor 03
Accountability

You bear the consequences. Own the call, not the model. No one on the receiving end of a bad decision has ever been comforted by hearing which tool helped make it.

Ask: if this fails, whose name is on it?

Anchor 04
Truth

Verify the facts, ask for alternatives, and test what could be wrong before you act. Check any number, quote, or policy the model hands you, especially the ones that sound most quotable.

Ask: what could this be wrong about, and how would I know?

Authority, Purpose, Accountability, Truth. Four questions, fifteen seconds, every time.

Try it · 45 seconds

Which anchor did they skip?

Three managers, three near misses. Name the anchor that would have caught it.

1. A manager sends the AI’s draft announcement to the whole team without changing a word. Two people reply asking whether a human wrote it.

Authority. The draft left the building in the model’s voice instead of the manager’s. One editing pass would have fixed it, and the team would have heard their manager instead of a template.

2. A manager quotes a policy detail from an AI summary in a memo to their director. The policy changed last year and the summary was describing the old version.

Truth. A confident sentence about policy is exactly the kind of claim to check before it appears in a memo with your name on it. Confidence is the model’s style, not its evidence.

3. A manager runs a weekly report through AI, sends it out every Friday, and cannot say what anyone does with it. It has gone out for four months.

Purpose. Faster production of something nobody reads is not a win, it is the same waste at higher speed. If it does not advance a goal you can name, the right move is to stop sending it.

Together

Forty-five seconds, out loud, in pairs. Each person picks one AI output they generated this week and answers all four questions to their partner. The partner’s only job is to notice which answer came hardest.

Do it now · 45 seconds

Pick any AI output you generated this week and answer these out loud: Am I editing this in my voice? Which goal does this serve? If it fails, whose name is on it? What could this be wrong about, and how would I know? The one that is hardest to answer is the one worth your next five minutes.
