Wider options before the call.

AI is an extremely persuasive summarizer, and that is the risk. It can argue any side well, which means a strong case for the option you already liked tells you almost nothing. Use it to widen what you can see and to attack your own plan. Then apply judgment, because the judgment is the part that was always yours.

Two prompts, in this order

The first prompt widens: it asks for options that are genuinely different from each other, not three shades of the same idea, plus an early signal you could watch for to tell whether each one is working.

The second prompt attacks: it assumes your decision already failed and asks for the retrospective. This is called a premortem, and it works because it is far easier to explain a failure that has happened than to imagine one that has not.

Prompt 01 · options and tradeoffs 
I am deciding whether to [decision]. Here is what I know: [context].
The stakeholders are: [list them by role]. Constraints: [budget, timeline, policy].

Give me:
1. Three distinct options. Not variations on one idea.
2. For each: the strongest case, the biggest risk, and one signal
   I could look for in the next 30 days to know if it is working.
3. One option I probably have not considered.

End with the three questions I should be able to answer before I decide.
Prompt 02 · the premortem 
Assume the decision I just described failed badly, six months from now.
Write the memo my leadership would send explaining what went wrong.

Then work backward: what were the three earliest warning signs we ignored?
What would a version of me who saw them coming have done differently?

Run this one second, on the option you are leaning toward. Read it twice. It is the part managers skip, and it is the part that pays.

Try it · 30 seconds

The output is in. What is your next move?

1. You asked for three options and the model came back strongly recommending option A, with a confident case.

Ask for the counter-case. The model can argue any side persuasively, so a strong case for A tells you little until you have read the strongest case against it. Once you have both, the call is yours to make and yours to own.

2. The premortem names a failure you cannot rule out. What does that finding become?

It becomes a to-check item. A premortem finding is not a veto and it is not paperwork. Every failure reason you cannot rule out is either a question to answer now or a signal to put on your calendar for later.

Together

Take one real open decision from the room, described by role only. The instructor runs the options prompt live, then the premortem. Tables split the output: one takes the warning signs, one takes the option nobody considered, one takes the three questions. Each reports back in a sentence.

Pause + reflect

Name the decision that has been sitting on your desk longest. Then answer one question honestly: are you waiting because it is genuinely hard, or because you have not yet seen the option that makes it easy? The first prompt is for the second case.
