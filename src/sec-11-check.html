Four questions, and none of them are tricks. Knowledge check / 11

What you’ll remember.

Pick an answer on each and you will see right away how you did. Nothing here is scored or sent anywhere. If the version of this course in your learning system includes a graded check, that one replaces this.

1. You are about to paste something into an AI tool and you cannot remember how it is classified. What do you do?

Amplify, and stop if you suspect it is Level 4, PHI, or HIPAA. Deleting a chat afterwards does not undo the paste, and uncertainty about classification is itself the answer: it goes to the most protected place you have, or nowhere at all.

2. Two prompts for the same announcement. Which one gets the more useful draft?

The second. It carries all five parts of the pattern, and the clarifying line makes the model ask before it writes. The question it asks is usually the part of the announcement you had not decided yet, which is worth more than the draft.

3. A manager pastes a team member’s performance review into an AI tool to think through a role change. What went wrong?

Rule one, the strictest in the program. Approved tools are for operational detail, not personal records, and no prompt fix makes a performance review safe to paste. Take the matter to HR. If you use AI to think about the situation at all, describe it as a role with nothing that could identify anyone.

4. The AI hands you a polished draft that reads well and sounds nothing like you. Which anchor is about to be tested?

Authority. Polish is not the same as ownership, and a team can tell the difference between a message from their manager and a message from a template. One editing pass is the whole fix. Truth still needs its own pass on any number or policy in there.

The finish line. Recap / 12

Fifteen minutes, five moves, one frame.

This course is a starting line. The managers who lead best with these tools keep their judgment loud, their voice present, and their team at the center. Everything below fits on one printed page.

You learned

One frame

Authority, Purpose, Accountability, Truth. Four questions, fifteen seconds, before any output becomes an action. Truth is the one to lean on hardest, because confident writing is the model’s default and accuracy is not6.

You learned

One pattern

Role, Context, Task, Constraints, Format, and the line that makes it work: ask me one clarifying question before you draft. It writes announcements, agendas, coaching prep, and the opening of a hard conversation.

You learned

One rule about data

Data classification decides. Level 4, PHI, HIPAA, and anything about children go into no tool at all. Levels 1 to 3 go to Amplify when you are unsure2. People are described by role, never by name.

Do these three things
  1. Use your habit three times this week. Pick one in Module 05 if you have not yet. Three uses is roughly where a habit stops needing a decision.
  2. Print your takeaway. One page with the anchors, the pattern, your prompt, your habit, every module prompt, and the guardrails.
  3. Book fifteen minutes with yourself two weeks out. Two questions: did the habit stick, and did every draft still sound like you?
